A post-mortem is read by someone who was not there and will not ask a follow-up
question. It earns its length by being specific about time and cost.

Readers come for: what was observed, what it cost the people on the other end,
why it stayed hidden, what actually happened, what changed, and what would catch
it sooner. The order is negotiable. Omitting the cost is not — a post-mortem
without it reads as an engineering anecdote.

Numbers belong near the front. A fix stated without the detection gap leaves the
reader with nothing to apply.

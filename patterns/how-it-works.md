An explanation is judged by whether the reader can predict behaviour afterwards,
not by how much ground it covers.

Start from the thing the reader already believes and name where that belief
breaks. Introduce one mechanism at a time, and give each one a case where it
visibly matters. Skip the history unless the design only makes sense with it.

A worked example beats a definition. If a paragraph could be deleted without the
reader losing the ability to predict anything, delete it.
